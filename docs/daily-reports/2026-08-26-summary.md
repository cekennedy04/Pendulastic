* **Errors & Troubleshooting:**
  * **Gyro dt fallback:** the guard `0.0 < dt < 0.5` silently substituted 10 ms for any gyro stream slower than 2 Hz. Sensor Stream Pro delivers gyro at 1 Hz (accel at 100 Hz), so a ~90° rotation was reported as 4.9°. Bound widened to 2.0 s so measured dt is actually used.
  * **Gyro-bias calibration was subtracting its own noise:** a 1 s hold-window estimate erred by 3× the true bias, injecting 23° of yaw drift. An axis's bias is now applied only when its mean is ≥3 standard errors from zero (2σ accepts 8.7% of axes on pure noise; 3σ accepts 0.3%).
  * **Unobservable yaw reported as knee flexion:** with no flexion axis captured, the fallback returned total quaternion distance including free-integrating yaw — a still phone reported 86.9° of flexion. The fallback now projects the rotation axis perpendicular to the vertical captured at zero(): 23.2° → 0.6° on replay.
  * **Stillness threshold unit bug:** `ACCEL_STATIONARY_MAX_MPS2 = 0.18` was actually derived from a g-unit corpus, so it was correct for g devices and 9.81× too strict for m/s² ones. Renamed to `ACCEL_STATIONARY_MAX_G` and compared against the window's own measured gravity magnitude.
  * All fixes were pinned by tests confirmed to fail without them, and re-validated against the 71-trial OptiTrack corpus (no regression: mean RMSE 16.84 → 16.81).
* **Key Learnings:**
  * **Corpus validation ≠ confirmation.** All 71 archive trials ran gyro at exactly 10 ms and 70/71 captured a flexion axis — so the corpus *cannot exercise* the defects fixed today. The evidence was live phone trials; the corpus only shows no regression.
  * **A warning the operator can miss is not enforcement.** Three 1 Hz trials were recorded with the low-rate warning on screen; two were scored before anyone noticed. The gate now refuses to record.
  * **Misnamed constants propagate:** the `_MPS2` suffix on a g-unit threshold led a prior planning doc to assert the wrong units as a global constraint.
  * A peer review caught that the "longer windows don't help" bias claim was wrong — measured, error follows 1/√N, so a ~10 s hold would make the bias genuinely resolvable. Recorded next to the constant; changing the hold length is a clinical-protocol decision, not a constant tweak.
